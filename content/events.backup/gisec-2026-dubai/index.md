abstract: |
  Peer-to-peer (P2P) botnets present a unique cybersecurity challenge because they operate without relying on conventional centralized command-and-control infrastructure, making them inherently difficult to observe, track and disrupt. This workshop introduces participants to the lifecycle of monitoring and investigating P2P botnets, from discovering and infiltrating a botnet to collecting, analysing and interpreting observations from within the network. Drawing upon real-world botnet monitoring research and case studies, the session demonstrates what researchers and defenders can learn about botnet populations, behaviour and evolution, while also highlighting the practical challenges and limitations of monitoring malicious P2P networks.

  **Learning outcomes:** by the end of the workshop, participants will be able to explain how P2P botnets differ from traditional centralized botnets, understand the lifecycle and key techniques involved in monitoring them, recognise the types of threat intelligence obtainable through P2P botnet monitoring, understand the practical challenges and limitations of observing and measuring them, and appreciate how monitoring and intelligence contribute to the detection, investigation and mitigation of botnet threats.

  **Target audience:** cybersecurity researchers, practitioners, threat intelligence and SOC professionals, educators, students, and anyone interested in malware, botnets and Internet threat monitoring. A basic understanding of computer networking is helpful, but prior experience with botnet monitoring is not required.
